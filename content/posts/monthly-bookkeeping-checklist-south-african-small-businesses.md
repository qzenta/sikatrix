We wrote about [the difference between bookkeeping and accounting](/resources/bookkeeping-vs-accounting-sme-south-africa) recently, and one thing that came up repeatedly in follow-up questions was: okay, but what does "keeping the books current" actually involve, month by month? It's a fair question — "do your bookkeeping" is not a task, it's a category, and most small business owners doing it themselves have never seen it broken into an actual routine.

This is the checklist we'd hand a client who wants to do their own bookkeeping properly between our reviews, or who wants to understand exactly what their bookkeeper should be doing every month.

## Before the Month Starts: Have the Right System

This isn't a monthly task, but it's the precondition for everything below working. You need one of the following, consistently:

- A cloud accounting platform (Xero, QuickBooks Online, Sage) linked to your business bank account, or
- A dedicated spreadsheet system with a clear chart of accounts, if your transaction volume is genuinely low

If you're capturing transactions from memory at month-end instead of recording them as they happen, none of the steps below will be reliable. See our comparison of [cloud accounting vs desktop software](/resources/cloud-accounting-vs-desktop) if you're still deciding on a system.

## Weekly (Not Just Monthly)

A few things are worth doing weekly rather than batching to month-end, because the cost of catching an error compounds the longer it sits:

1. **Capture and file all invoices and receipts.** Every sale invoice issued and every expense receipt received, filed against the transaction the same week it happens — not scanned into a folder to sort out later.
2. **Follow up on overdue invoices.** A quick weekly glance at your debtors list catches a late payment while a phone call still fixes it, rather than a full month later.

## The Monthly Checklist

### 1. Complete all transaction capturing

Before anything else, every transaction for the month needs to be captured and correctly categorised — every sale, every expense, every bank transfer. Uncategorised or "miscellaneous" transactions left over from a rushed month should be your first target; they distort every report built from the data.

### 2. Bank reconciliation

Match every transaction on your bank statement to a transaction in your books, for every bank account and credit facility the business holds. Your books' closing balance must equal your actual bank balance at month-end. Any unreconciled item — a bank fee you forgot to capture, a duplicate entry, a payment that hasn't reflected — needs to be resolved before you move on. This is the single most important check in the entire process; if it doesn't balance, nothing downstream can be trusted.

### 3. Accounts receivable (debtors) review

- Confirm every invoice issued during the month has been recorded
- Update your aged debtors report and flag anything moving into 60+ or 90+ days overdue
- Follow up on overdue accounts, or hand the list to whoever owns collections
- Write off or provide for genuinely bad debts according to your policy, rather than letting them sit indefinitely as "current"

### 4. Accounts payable (creditors) review

- Confirm every supplier invoice received has been captured, not just the ones that have been paid
- Reconcile supplier statements against what's recorded in your books
- Review upcoming payment due dates against your cash position, so nothing is paid late (or paid twice)

### 5. Payroll reconciliation (if you employ staff)

- Confirm payroll for the month has been processed and posted to the books correctly
- Reconcile PAYE, UIF, and SDL amounts withheld against what's due on the EMP201
- Confirm the EMP201 has actually been submitted and paid by the 7th of the following month
- Check leave accruals and any commission or bonus calculations for the month are reflected correctly

See our breakdown of [PAYE, UIF, and SDL obligations](/resources/paye-uif-sdl-explained) if you need the underlying detail on what each of these actually requires.

### 6. VAT reconciliation (if you're VAT registered)

- Reconcile output VAT (VAT charged on sales) and input VAT (VAT paid on purchases) for the period against your VAT201 working papers
- Confirm every input VAT claim has a valid, compliant tax invoice — SARS can and does disallow claims without one
- If your VAT period has closed during the month, confirm the VAT201 was submitted and paid by the due date

### 7. Fixed assets and depreciation

- Record any assets purchased or disposed of during the month
- Apply the month's depreciation according to your fixed asset register
- Confirm the fixed asset register still matches what the business actually owns — this is easy to let drift and painful to reconstruct at year-end

### 8. Review the trial balance

Once everything above is captured, pull a trial balance and check it for anything that looks obviously wrong: an expense account with an unexpectedly large balance, a balance sheet account that shouldn't have moved, a debit and credit that don't sum to zero. This is your last checkpoint before the month's numbers are considered final.

### 9. Produce monthly management reports

At minimum, a small business should be looking at:

- An income statement (profit and loss) for the month and year-to-date
- A balance sheet as at month-end
- An aged debtors and aged creditors report
- A cash flow summary or bank balance trend

These don't need to be elaborate, but they need to exist and be reviewed by someone — the point of doing all the above is to actually look at what it tells you, not just file it.

### 10. Back up and lock the period

Once the month is reviewed and finalised, back up your data (most cloud platforms do this automatically) and lock the period if your software supports it, so historical transactions can't be edited without a deliberate, logged adjustment. This protects the integrity of everything you've just reconciled.

## Where This Checklist Breaks Down in Practice

The businesses that fall behind on this usually don't fail at any single step — they fall behind on cadence. Doing it properly for two months and then skipping a month under pressure means month three starts with a backlog, and backlogs are where errors hide. The realistic fix isn't trying harder in month three; it's building the routine so skipping isn't an option — either by blocking calendar time for it every month without exception, or by handing the recurring parts to a bookkeeper so it happens whether or not the business owner has capacity that week.
