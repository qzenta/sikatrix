For individuals and companies with a February year-end, the second provisional tax payment is the one that counts. It is your final estimate for the year, and SARS measures it against your actual taxable income when it assesses your return. Get it badly wrong and you pay a penalty on top of interest.

For the 2026/2027 tax year, the payment falls due at the end of February 2027. This article explains the date, how to estimate, what the penalties are, and what to do before year-end.

## When the second payment is due

SARS requires the second payment no later than the last business day of the year of assessment. For a February year-end, that is the last business day of February. 28 February 2027 is a Sunday, so the deadline is Friday 26 February 2027.

If your company has a different year-end, the second payment falls on the last day of your financial year, adjusted the same way.

## How the second estimate works

Your second IRP6 is an estimate of taxable income for the full year, not for six months. SARS calculates the payment as the estimated tax for the whole year, less:

- Employees' tax deducted for the full year
- Foreign tax credits
- Rebates and medical tax credits, for individuals
- The amount you paid for the first period in August

By February you have eleven months of actual figures. Use them. Management accounts that are up to date to the end of January are the single most useful thing you can bring to your accountant.

## The underestimation penalty

SARS levies a penalty under paragraph 20 of the Fourth Schedule if your second estimate is too low. The test depends on your actual taxable income.

**Taxable income of R1 million or less:** A penalty applies if your estimate is less than 90% of actual taxable income and also less than the basic amount. The penalty is 20% of the difference between the lesser of the tax on 90% of actual taxable income and the tax on the basic amount, and the employees' tax and provisional tax you paid by year-end.

**Taxable income above R1 million:** A penalty applies if your estimate is less than 80% of actual taxable income. The penalty is 20% of the difference between the tax on 80% of actual taxable income and the employees' tax and provisional tax you paid by year-end.

The basic amount is your taxable income as last assessed by SARS, less any taxable capital gain in that year. If you make your estimate more than 18 months after the end of the year that was last assessed, the basic amount must be increased by 8%.

For smaller taxpayers, the basic amount works as a safe harbour. For anyone whose income has grown strongly, or whose income is above R1 million, it does not protect you. Estimate properly.

## Late payment and late submission

A 10% penalty applies to any provisional tax paid late for the first or second period, under paragraph 27. The underestimation penalty for the second period is reduced by any late payment penalty for the same period, so you are not penalised twice for the same shortfall.

If you do not submit your second IRP6 at all, you are treated as having submitted an estimate of nil taxable income, unless you submit it within four months after year-end. A nil estimate almost guarantees an underestimation penalty.

Interest also runs on late payments, and on any underpayment once SARS assesses your return. SARS publishes the prescribed interest rate in the Government Gazette, and it changes from time to time.

## The third payment: a voluntary top-up

If you realise after year-end that your second estimate was too low, you can make a voluntary third payment. For a February year-end, it is due by the last business day of September 2027. It does not remove an underestimation penalty, but it reduces the interest SARS charges on the shortfall.

## Year-end checklist for SMEs

Use November to January to prepare, so February is about checking rather than scrambling.

- Bring bookkeeping up to date and reconcile every bank account to the end of January
- Review debtors and write off amounts you genuinely cannot recover
- Count stock at year-end and value it consistently
- Check fixed asset additions and disposals for wear and tear claims
- Confirm director and shareholder loan balances
- Compare your August estimate with actual results and update it
- Set aside the cash for the February payment now, not in the last week

## Plan the payment, not just the estimate

The February payment often lands at the same time as the January EMP201 and, for many businesses, a VAT payment. Look at your cash flow for February now. If the numbers are tight, talk to your accountant early. There is far more room to plan in November than in the last week of February.
