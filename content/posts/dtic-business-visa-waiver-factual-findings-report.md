South Africa's Business Visa requires a minimum capital investment of R5 million. Most applicants who don't meet that threshold assume they're out of options. They're not — but the route to a lower figure runs through a specific, document-heavy process at the Department of Trade, Industry and Competition (DTIC), and the paperwork has to be exactly right the first time.

This article breaks down what the DTIC waiver actually is, what a Factual Findings Report involves, and — a question we get often and one that catches people out — what changes if you're renewing rather than applying for the first time.

## What the DTIC Waiver Actually Is

Every Business Visa application starts with a **DTIC recommendation** — the department's endorsement that your proposed business is economically viable and in South Africa's national interest. Home Affairs relies heavily on this recommendation; without it, an application generally doesn't proceed.

If your available capital is below the standard R5 million threshold, you don't automatically fail. Certain sectors — defined as national-interest industries — qualify for a **reduction or full waiver** of the capital requirement. If your business falls into one of these categories, you can apply for the waiver alongside (or immediately after) your DTIC recommendation.

The waiver application itself goes to the Department of Home Affairs, supported by the DTIC's recommendation. It is scrutinised more heavily than a standard application — the absence of capital has to be offset by a clear, well-evidenced case for the business's economic value. This is not a box-ticking exercise; it's an application that lives or dies on the quality of the financial documentation behind it.

**A timing note worth planning around:** the waiver step alone can take up to 12 months to process. If your capital sits close to R5 million, it is often faster in practice to structure the investment to clear the threshold outright rather than go through the waiver route. We discuss this trade-off with clients during the initial consultation.

## The Four Documents DTIC Requires

This is the part most applicants — and the immigration attorneys assisting them — get wrong, because the terminology is unfamiliar outside accounting circles. Here's what each document actually is.

### 1. Factual Findings Report

This is not an audit and not a review. It's an **Agreed-Upon Procedures (AUP) engagement**, performed under International Standard on Related Services (ISRS) 4400, where a registered accountant carries out a specific set of procedures agreed with DTIC and reports only the factual results — no opinion, no assurance conclusion. In practice, this means verifying your available capital, the source of those funds, and the figures in your business plan against the supporting evidence you provide.

### 2. Proof of Accountant's Professional Registration

The report has to come from a licensed South African accountant — a Chartered Accountant, Professional Accountant, or Business Accountant registered with SAICA, SAIPA, or CIBA. Proof of that registration is submitted alongside the report itself, so DTIC can confirm the person signing it is entitled to do so.

### 3. Representation Letter

This is signed by you, the applicant — not the accountant. It's a formal written confirmation that the information you've provided for the factual findings procedures is complete and accurate. It's a standard requirement of any ISRS 4400 engagement, and DTIC will not accept the factual findings report without it.

### 4. Proof of Assets

The supporting evidence — bank statements, asset valuations, financial statements — that substantiates the capital figure the factual findings report is testing against. This is the foundation everything else is built on, so it needs to be current, complete, and consistent with what's stated in your business plan.

## What's Different for a Renewal

This is where most guidance online falls short, because almost everything written about the Business Visa assumes a first-time application. Renewal is not a simple extension.

**To renew a Business Visa, you essentially have to re-initiate the entire process:**

- **A new DTIC recommendation** — your original recommendation does not carry forward.
- **A fresh capital waiver application** — if you held a waiver originally, it has to be re-applied for, not automatically renewed.
- **A new Business Visa extension application** to Home Affairs.

The factual findings report for a renewal also does more work than it did the first time round. On a first application, the report is essentially a snapshot of available capital. On a renewal, DTIC and Home Affairs want to see that the capital has actually been deployed as promised and that the business has been operating — so expect the engagement to extend into your **trading history**, typically supported by **audited or reviewed financial statements for the previous two years**, not just current proof of funds.

Practically, that means starting your renewal paperwork well before your existing visa expires. Between the factual findings procedures, gathering two years of financials, and the DTIC's own processing time, this is not a process to begin in the final weeks of validity.
