Payroll is one of the few areas where SARS sees your numbers every single month. Errors do not wait for year-end to surface. They show up in your EMP201, then again in your EMP501 reconciliations, and they can block a tax clearance certificate when you need one for a tender.

This checklist sets out the payroll obligations every South African employer must meet, in the order they come around.

## Every month: EMP201

The EMP201 is your monthly employer declaration. It reports and pays three amounts together: PAYE deducted from employees, UIF contributions and the Skills Development Levy.

The EMP201 and payment are due by the 7th of the month after the payroll month. If the 7th falls on a weekend or public holiday, the deadline is the last business day before it. For example, 7 November 2026 is a Saturday, so the October 2026 EMP201 is due by Friday 6 November.

Your monthly checklist:

- Run payroll and check every new employee, termination and pay change
- Confirm PAYE has been calculated on the current tax tables
- Check UIF and SDL totals before you submit
- Submit the EMP201 and make the payment using the correct payment reference
- Keep the payroll report and proof of payment with the month's records

## UIF: what to deduct

UIF is 1% of the employee's remuneration, deducted from their pay, plus a matching 1% from the employer. The total is 2%.

Contributions are capped at a remuneration ceiling of R17,712 a month (R212,544 a year), so the maximum deduction is R177.12 a month from the employee, matched by the employer. Employers registered with SARS for PAYE or SDL pay UIF through the EMP201.

## SDL: does it apply to you?

The Skills Development Levy is 1% of the total amount paid in salaries, including overtime and bonuses. It is paid within seven days after the end of the month, on the same EMP201.

You are exempt from SDL if you expect your total salaries over the next 12 months to stay at or below R500,000. Watch this threshold if you are hiring. Once you expect to go above it, you must register for SDL.

Our guide to [PAYE, UIF and SDL](/resources/paye-uif-sdl-explained) covers each of these in more detail.

## Twice a year: EMP501 reconciliations

The EMP501 reconciles what you declared and paid on your monthly EMP201s with the tax certificates (IRP5 and IT3(a)) you issue to employees. SARS requires two reconciliations each year.

**Interim reconciliation:** Covers 1 March to 31 August. SARS's calendar gives the submission period as 1 September to 31 October. For 2026, SARS opened the employer interim filing season on 21 September 2026, with the window ending on 31 October 2026.

**Annual reconciliation:** Covers the full tax year, 1 March to the end of February. SARS's calendar gives the submission period as 1 April to 31 May. The annual reconciliation for the 2026/2027 year (1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027) is therefore expected from 1 April to 31 May 2027. SARS confirms the exact window each year, so check it in March.

Before you submit either reconciliation:

- Check that the EMP201 totals for each month match the payments SARS has allocated
- Confirm every employee has a valid ID or passport number and an income tax number where required
- Check IRP5 source codes, especially for allowances and fringe benefits
- Fix any differences before submission, not after SARS queries them

Our [EMP501 annual reconciliation guide](/resources/emp501-annual-reconciliation-guide-south-africa) walks through the process step by step.

## Once a year: COIDA Return of Earnings

Employers registered with the Compensation Fund must submit an annual Return of Earnings (ROE). It declares actual earnings for the previous assessment year and provisional earnings for the current one. The Fund uses it to calculate your assessment.

The ROE deadline is 30 June. For 2026, the Compensation Fund gazetted a submission window of 1 April to 30 June 2026 in Notice No. 3894 of 2026. Plan to file in April or May so a technical problem on the online system does not push you past the deadline.

For more detail, see our [COIDA Return of Earnings guide](/resources/coida-return-of-earnings-guide-south-africa).

## The full-year payroll calendar at a glance

- Every month by the 7th (or the last business day before it): EMP201 and payment
- 1 April to 31 May: EMP501 annual reconciliation for the tax year just ended
- 1 April to 30 June: COIDA Return of Earnings
- September to 31 October: EMP501 interim reconciliation for March to August
- End of February: tax year ends, so finalise payroll records and prepare IRP5 certificates

For every date in the 2026/2027 year, including VAT and provisional tax, see our [SARS compliance calendar tool](/tools/sars-compliance-calendar).

## Common payroll mistakes we see

The same problems come up again and again with new clients. Casual and part-time workers are left off the payroll. Directors are paid without PAYE being deducted. Allowances are coded incorrectly, which distorts the IRP5. Payments are made with the wrong reference, so SARS cannot allocate them. Each of these is easy to fix early and painful to fix at reconciliation time.
