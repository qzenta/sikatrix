New VAT thresholds took effect on 1 April 2026: the compulsory registration threshold rose from R1 million to R2.3 million, and the voluntary threshold rose from R50,000 to R120,000. In this episode, we unpack what that actually means for a growing South African SME.

## What Changed

Following the 2026 Budget, SARS raised both VAT registration thresholds effective 1 April 2026 — the compulsory threshold from R1 million to R2.3 million in taxable supplies over any rolling 12-month period, and the voluntary threshold from R50,000 to R120,000. The underlying mechanism hasn't changed: it's still a rolling 12-month test, not a calendar-year reset, so a business can cross the threshold mid-year without realising it until the numbers are checked.

## Why Voluntary Registration Is Worth a Second Look

Registering before you're forced to isn't just an administrative head start. If your business carries significant VAT-inclusive costs, or your clients are mostly other VAT-registered businesses, voluntary registration under the new R120,000 threshold can be a genuine cash-flow advantage rather than a burden taken on early.

## Why SARS Is Already Auto-Registering Businesses

This isn't a future risk — SARS's automatic data-matching system is already pulling bank and CIPC data and actively catching businesses that cross the compulsory threshold, sometimes registering them without an application ever being submitted, and sometimes with a backdated effective date that can mean owing VAT, penalties, and interest for periods never charged for.
